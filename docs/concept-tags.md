# Concept Tags — Controlled Vocabulary

This file lists every tag that appears in `conceptTags` fields across the question bank.
Every tag used in a question YAML must appear here. First use of a tag adds it with a
one-line definition. Reuse enforces consistency.

Maintain alphabetical order within each section.

---

## Crystallography & Crystal Systems

| Tag | Definition |
|-----|-----------|
| `biaxial` | Optical character where a material has two optic axes and three principal refractive indices (alpha, beta, gamma); found in orthorhombic, monoclinic, and triclinic minerals |
| `crystal-habit` | The characteristic external shape and face development of a crystal species |
| `crystal-system` | Classification of crystals into one of seven systems (cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, monoclinic, triclinic, hexagonal, trigonal) based on axial geometry and symmetry |
| `cubic-system` | Highest-symmetry crystal system with four threefold axes and all axes equal at 90°; minerals are isotropic |
| `hexagonal-system` | Crystal system with one sixfold rotation axis; uniaxial optic character |
| `isotropic` | Optical property of materials (cubic system, amorphous) that transmit light equally in all directions; single refractive index, no birefringence |
| `miller-indices` | Notation {hkl} or {hkil} describing orientation of crystal faces by reciprocals of fractional intercepts on crystallographic axes |
| `monoclinic-system` | Crystal system with one twofold axis or mirror plane; biaxial optic character |
| `optic-axis` | Direction(s) in an anisotropic crystal along which light travels without double refraction |
| `orthorhombic-system` | Crystal system with three mutually perpendicular twofold axes; biaxial optic character |
| `parting` | Tendency to split along twinning planes, resembling cleavage but contingent on twinning; not universal in a species |
| `pleochroism` | Absorption of different wavelengths in different crystallographic directions in anisotropic minerals, producing apparent colour change on rotation |
| `point-group` | The set of symmetry operations (rotation axes, mirror planes, inversion centre) leaving at least one point unmoved; 32 crystallographic point groups exist |
| `symmetry` | The set of symmetry elements (axes, planes, centre) characterising a crystal class; determines physical properties including optical character |
| `tetragonal-system` | Crystal system with one fourfold rotation axis; uniaxial optic character |
| `triclinic-system` | Lowest-symmetry crystal system with no rotational symmetry; biaxial optic character |
| `trigonal-system` | Crystal system with one threefold rotation axis; uniaxial optic character; shares axial geometry with hexagonal |
| `twinning` | Intergrowth of two or more crystal individuals related by a symmetry operation not present in the single crystal |
| `uniaxial` | Optical character where a material has one optic axis and two principal refractive indices (omega and epsilon); found in hexagonal, trigonal, and tetragonal minerals |

## Optical Properties & Refractometer

| Tag | Definition |
|-----|-----------|
| `birefringence` | Numerical difference between the maximum and minimum refractive indices of an anisotropic gem; equals DR on the refractometer |
| `critical-angle` | The angle of incidence above which total internal reflection occurs at an interface; the refractometer measures RI via this angle |
| `fluorescence` | Emission of visible light by a gem when irradiated with UV light; ceases when UV source is removed |
| `fluorescence-LWUV` | Fluorescence response under long-wave ultraviolet light (365 nm) |
| `fluorescence-SWUV` | Fluorescence response under short-wave ultraviolet light (254 nm) |
| `optic-character` | Classification of a gem as isotropic, uniaxial, or biaxial based on its interaction with polarised light |
| `optic-sign` | For uniaxial: positive if epsilon > omega; negative if epsilon < omega. For biaxial: positive if beta is closer to alpha, negative if closer to gamma |
| `refractive-index` | The ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to the speed in the gem material; the principal diagnostic optical property |
| `sturman-patterns` | Darko Sturman's classification of seven observable shadow-edge behaviour patterns on the refractometer during stone rotation |
| `total-internal-reflection` | Reflection of light at a boundary when travelling from denser to less dense medium at angles exceeding the critical angle; the operating principle of the refractometer |

## Physical Properties

| Tag | Definition |
|-----|-----------|
| `cleavage` | The tendency of a mineral to break along flat planes parallel to crystallographic directions of weaker bonding; a universal property of the species |
| `dispersion` | The variation of refractive index with wavelength; measured in gemmology as n(G) - n(B); produces "fire" in faceted stones |
| `fracture` | The way a mineral breaks in directions other than cleavage planes; types include conchoidal, uneven, splintery |
| `hardness` | Resistance to scratching, measured on the Mohs scale from 1 (talc) to 10 (diamond) |
| `lustre` | The appearance of light reflected from a mineral surface; types include adamantine, vitreous, resinous, waxy, pearly |
| `magnetism` | Response of a gem to a strong magnet; paramagnetic gems with high Fe or Mn content show measurable attraction |
| `specific-gravity` | Ratio of a gem's mass to the mass of an equal volume of water; measured by hydrostatic weighing (SG = W_air / (W_air - W_water)) |
| `tenacity` | Resistance to mechanical deformation: brittle, tough, sectile, malleable, flexible, elastic |
| `thermal-conductivity` | Rate at which a material conducts heat; diagnostically high in diamond and moissanite |

## Inclusions & Internal Features

| Tag | Definition |
|-----|-----------|
| `anomalous-double-refraction` | Strain birefringence observed in nominally isotropic (cubic) gems such as garnets under the polariscope; appears as patchy light/dark patterns |
| `fingerprint-inclusion` | Healed fracture with a network of tiny fluid remnants resembling a human fingerprint pattern |
| `flux-fingerprint` | Fingerprint-type inclusion formed during high-temperature flux-assisted treatment; appears fuzzy or cotton-like compared to natural fingerprints |
| `growth-tube` | Hollow or fluid-filled tubular channel parallel to the growth direction of a crystal; common in tourmaline |
| `needle-inclusion` | Elongated, rod-shaped solid inclusion within a gem; may be rutile, tourmaline, actinolite, or other minerals |
| `negative-crystal` | Fluid-filled cavity with the geometric shape of the host mineral's own crystal form |
| `silk-inclusion` | Fine rutile (TiO2) needle inclusions exsolved in corundum, oriented at 60° intersections; key diagnostic and origin indicator |
| `three-phase-inclusion` | Fluid inclusion containing three distinct phases simultaneously: liquid, gas bubble, and a solid crystal (e.g. halite cube in Colombian emerald) |
| `two-phase-inclusion` | Fluid inclusion containing liquid and a mobile gas bubble; confirms natural origin in many species |
| `zircon-halo` | Circular stress fracture surrounding a zircon crystal inclusion, caused by radiation damage expanding the zircon lattice |

## Species Identification

| Tag | Definition |
|-----|-----------|
| `almandine` | Iron-aluminium garnet; cubic; RI 1.77-1.83; SG 3.9-4.2; deep red; often shows anomalous double refraction |
| `beryl` | Hexagonal beryllium aluminium silicate; uniaxial negative; includes emerald, aquamarine, morganite |
| `chrysoberyl` | Orthorhombic BeAl2O4; biaxial positive; includes alexandrite and cat's-eye varieties |
| `corundum` | Trigonal Al2O3; uniaxial negative; RI 1.762-1.770; SG 3.99-4.01; hardness 9; includes ruby and sapphire |
| `demantoid` | Andradite (calcium iron garnet); cubic; RI ~1.89 (above refractometer range); highest dispersion of natural garnets (0.057) |
| `diamond` | Cubic carbon; isotropic; RI 2.417; SG 3.52; hardness 10; adamantine lustre |
| `emerald` | Green variety of beryl coloured by Cr3+ and/or V3+; uniaxial negative; RI 1.577-1.583; SG 2.67-2.78 |
| `glass` | Amorphous silicate simulant; isotropic; single RI (variable 1.44-1.70); SG ~2.5; no birefringence; conchoidal fracture |
| `peridot` | Gem variety of olivine (Mg,Fe)2SiO4; orthorhombic; biaxial positive; RI 1.654-1.690; SG 3.28-3.48; hardness 6.5-7 |
| `pyrope` | Magnesium-aluminium garnet; cubic; RI 1.730-1.760; SG 3.65-3.87; deep red; isotropic |
| `quartz` | Trigonal SiO2; uniaxial positive; RI 1.544-1.553; SG 2.65; hardness 7 |
| `ruby` | Red corundum coloured by Cr3+; uniaxial negative; RI 1.762-1.770; SG 4.00; strong red fluorescence LWUV |
| `sapphire` | Non-red corundum; uniaxial negative; RI 1.762-1.770; SG 4.00; blue coloured by Fe-Ti charge transfer |
| `spinel` | Cubic MgAl2O4; isotropic; RI 1.712-1.720; SG 3.58-3.61; hardness 8 |
| `synthetic-ruby` | Lab-grown corundum with ruby composition; same RI and SG as natural ruby but with diagnostic growth features (curved striae in flame fusion; flux fingerprints in flux-grown) |
| `topaz` | Orthorhombic Al2SiO4(F,OH)2; biaxial positive; RI 1.619-1.627; SG 3.49-3.57; perfect basal cleavage {001} |
| `tourmaline` | Trigonal borosilicate; uniaxial negative; RI 1.620-1.640; SG 3.06; strong pleochroism; distinct absorption spectrum |
| `tsavorite` | Green grossular garnet coloured by Cr and V; cubic; RI ~1.74; SG ~3.61; strong green; no fluorescence |
| `zircon` | Tetragonal ZrSiO4; uniaxial positive; RI 1.931-1.984 (high type); SG 4.69; strong birefringence; adamantine lustre |

## Treatments & Synthetics

| Tag | Definition |
|-----|-----------|
| `flux-growth` | Synthetic crystal growth method using a molten salt flux medium; produces characteristic flux fingerprints and curved or irregular growth features |
| `heat-treatment` | Thermal processing of gems to improve colour or clarity; detectable via dissolution of silk, altered fingerprints, burst fluid inclusions |
| `hydrothermal-growth` | Synthetic crystal growth in hot water under pressure; produces characteristic chevron or curved growth patterns |
| `natural-vs-synthetic` | Discrimination between natural and laboratory-grown gems of the same species using inclusions, growth features, and spectroscopic tests |
